# Organic nomenclature

Use school-level, recognisable 9701 conventions, not preferred-IUPAC maximalism. A name can be recognised, conventional, or pedagogically useful even when another systematic synonym exists.

## Allowed surface normalisation

- Restore obvious hyphens in unambiguous spoken locants: “two methyl propane” → `2-methylpropane` when the phrase is plainly the name, not a naming discussion.
- Use conventional lower-case chemical names in running caption text, except normal sentence capitalization.
- Keep explicit locants, multiplicative prefixes, and functional-group suffixes spoken by the teacher.
- Record aliases as aliases rather than corrections: `ethanoic acid` / `acetic acid`, `propanone` / `acetone`, `methylbenzene` / `toluene`.

## Preserve instead

- “Acetic acid is the common name; ethanoic acid is systematic.” Protect each name and its name-status label separately; use a hint for the contrast.
- A teacher's offered alternative name, even if its status is uncertain, is preserved with a warning rather than silently replaced.
- “The methyl group is on the second carbon” is not automatically a fully resolved systematic name unless the parent and structure are grounded.
- Do not canonicalise a name when it is explicitly negated, quoted as an incorrect alternative, being corrected, or being compared as language. In “This is two bromo propane, not two propanol,” `2-bromopropane` may be canonicalised as the positive assertion, while “not two propanol” remains intact. Do not turn the rejected phrase into a different apparently valid name.

Protected phrases must stay minimal. In a name comparison, protect each name and each name-status term; express the relationship with a `comparison` or `teacher-correction` hint rather than protecting the full sentence.

## Do not infer

- a parent chain, locants, branching, functional-group priority, or stereochemical descriptor from a molecular formula or a partial name;
- a structural isomer from `C4H10`, `C4H8`, or another molecular formula alone;
- whether a common-name use is an error just because it is not the requested systematic form.

See `data/nomenclature.yaml` for caption-oriented aliases and spelling variants; it is a recognition aid, not a chemical correctness oracle.
